# Acme Systems — Data Protection and Records Policy (v4.2)

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs the collection, processing, storage, transfer and disposal of personal data by Acme Systems Ltd and every subsidiary, contractor and processor acting on its behalf. It applies to all data subjects whose personal data Acme processes, irrespective of their location, and to all Acme personnel regardless of employment status. Failure to comply with this policy is a disciplinary matter and may, where the failure results in a regulatory finding, give rise to personal liability for the individuals concerned.

The obligations set out in sections 4 through 11 are mandatory. Where a section is expressed as guidance rather than obligation, it is labelled as such. Any exception must be recorded in the exceptions register and approved in writing by the Data Protection Officer before the relevant processing begins.

## 2. Roles and responsibilities

Acme has appointed a Data Protection Officer (DPO) who reports directly to the Board and who may not be dismissed or penalised for performing their tasks. The DPO maintains the record of processing activities, advises on data protection impact assessments, and acts as the contact point for supervisory authorities.

Each business unit nominates a Data Steward responsible for the accuracy and lawfulness of processing within that unit. Data Stewards must complete refresher training annually and must notify the DPO of any new processing activity at least fourteen days before it commences.

## 3. Lawful basis and consent

No personal data may be processed without a documented lawful basis. Where consent is the chosen basis, that consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, and must be capable of withdrawal by the data subject at any time without detriment. Pre-ticked boxes, silence and inactivity do not constitute consent. Records of consent must show what the data subject was told, when, and by what means they signified agreement.

Consent obtained from a child under the age of sixteen is valid only where it is given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility. Services directed at children must present privacy information in language a child of that age can reasonably be expected to understand. Where Acme has reasonable doubt as to a data subject's age, it must apply the protections applicable to children.

## 4. Data minimisation and retention

Personal data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the purpose for which it is processed. Collecting data because it may prove useful in future is expressly prohibited.

Retention periods are set out in the retention schedule maintained by the DPO. In summary: transaction records are retained for seven years from the end of the financial year in which the transaction completed; recruitment records for unsuccessful candidates are retained for twelve months; marketing contact data is retained for twenty-four months from the last meaningful interaction; server access logs are retained for ninety days; and CCTV footage is retained for thirty-one days. On expiry of the applicable period, data must be securely erased or irreversibly anonymised. Deletion must be evidenced in the disposal log.

Backup media are exempt from immediate erasure but must be subject to a documented rolling overwrite cycle not exceeding six months. Where a deletion request cannot be actioned against a backup, the data must be placed beyond use and erased at the next overwrite.

## 5. Security of processing

All personal data at rest in Acme systems must be encrypted using AES-256 or an algorithm of equivalent or greater strength approved by the Security Architecture Group. Data in transit must be protected with TLS 1.2 or above; plaintext transport of personal data across any network, including internal networks, is prohibited.

Access to personal data is granted on a least-privilege basis and reviewed quarterly. Privileged access requires multi-factor authentication. Shared accounts are prohibited for any system holding personal data.

Comprehensive audit logging is mandatory for every system in scope. Logs must record the identity of the actor, the time of the action, the records affected and the outcome. Audit logs must themselves be protected against alteration and must be retained for a minimum of one year. Absence of audit logging is treated as a critical control failure.

## 6. Personal data breaches

A personal data breach is any breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data. All suspected breaches must be reported to the DPO immediately and in any event within twenty-four hours of discovery, using the breach report form.

Where a breach is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, Acme must notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than seventy-two hours after having become aware of it. Where the risk is high, the affected data subjects must also be informed without undue delay, in clear and plain language.

Failure to report a breach within the internal twenty-four hour window is a disciplinary offence. Regulatory penalties for late notification may reach the higher of twenty million euro or four per cent of global annual turnover.

## 7. International transfers

Personal data may not be transferred outside the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area unless one of the following applies: the destination benefits from an adequacy decision; the transfer is governed by executed standard contractual clauses supplemented by a documented transfer risk assessment; or binding corporate rules approved by the relevant authority are in force.

Transfers to processors in jurisdictions with laws permitting bulk government access must be accompanied by supplementary technical measures, ordinarily end-to-end encryption under keys held exclusively within the UK or EEA. The DPO maintains the register of approved transfer mechanisms and must approve each new transfer route before data flows.

## 8. Rights of data subjects

Acme recognises and will give effect to the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection, together with rights relating to automated decision-making.

Requests must be acknowledged within three working days and substantively answered within one calendar month of receipt. That period may be extended by a further two months where the request is complex or numerous, provided the data subject is informed of the extension and the reasons for it within the first month.

The right to erasure is not absolute. Where Acme declines to erase, it must record the exemption relied upon and inform the data subject of their right to complain to the supervisory authority and to seek a judicial remedy. Erasure requests must be propagated to processors and, where the data has been made public, reasonable steps must be taken to inform other controllers processing that data.

## 9. Processors and supply chain

No personal data may be disclosed to a third party acting as a processor without a written contract meeting the requirements of applicable data protection law. The contract must bind the processor to act only on documented instructions, to impose confidentiality obligations on its personnel, to implement appropriate security measures, to assist with data subject rights and breach notification, and to delete or return data at the end of the engagement.

Sub-processing requires prior written authorisation. Processors must be assessed before engagement and reassessed at least every two years, or sooner where the nature of the processing changes materially.

## 10. Data protection impact assessments

A data protection impact assessment is mandatory before commencing processing that is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, including systematic and extensive profiling with legal or similarly significant effects, large-scale processing of special category data, and systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas on a large scale.

Where an assessment indicates a high residual risk that cannot be mitigated, the DPO must consult the supervisory authority before processing begins.

## 11. Training and assurance

All personnel must complete data protection training on induction and annually thereafter. Completion is tracked and non-completion is escalated to the relevant line manager after thirty days.

Internal audit will test compliance with this policy at least annually and will report findings to the Board. Business units must respond to audit findings with a remediation plan within twenty working days.

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed annually by the DPO and following any material change in law, regulatory guidance or Acme's processing activities. The version history is maintained in the document management system.
